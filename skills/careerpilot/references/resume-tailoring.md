# Resume tailoring: evidence first, employer priorities next

Read for resume authoring and revision, together with document-quality.md for helper
contracts. The host writes and reviews; no separate model service is required. The
objective is a clear, credible case for an interview, not maximum keyword density.
Never promise a pass rate, interview, or an employer's ATS score. Do not impersonate
an experienced recruiter or repeat unsourced resume-rejection statistics.

## 1. Establish the target and constraints

Use the selected job's complete posting and current confirmed profile/import sources.
Reuse saved inputs; do not request a resume already available. If the posting is
missing or truncated, obtain it or label the result a general draft with tailoring
pending. Read approved guidance and workspace document rules. A pasted prompt's
one-page example is not a candidate instruction to change every future resume.

Follow an explicit page/format preference. Otherwise choose length to accommodate
relevant evidence without shrinking text excessively. Keep official job titles,
employers, dates and qualifications unchanged. A target-role summary must not imply
that the candidate already held that title. Never infer seniority from a target title.

## 2. Build the requirement-to-evidence map

Review the full posting, including eligibility, essential qualifications and preferred
criteria. Separate must-haves, preferences and responsibilities using the posting's
wording; label your interpretation when priority is ambiguous. Identify the employer's
three strongest stated needs, or fewer when the posting does not support three.

For each material requirement, retain its exact quote, priority, best profile/import
source, candidate's actual contribution, and where evidence will appear in the resume.
Classify it as covered, gap or unknown using the existing verification contract.
Partial or adjacent experience is not full coverage: state what is supported and what
is missing in the reason; use gap or unknown for the unmet portion. Split compound
requirements where possible without turning a partial quote into a different demand.

Distinguish possession of a qualification from evidence of applying it. Skills listed
without examples can support a skills entry, but not a fabricated project or outcome.
A missing essential qualification remains visible even when many preferences match.

Ask a small, batched set of questions only when answers could materially improve fit
or resolve ambiguity: personal ownership, tools used, deliverable, scope, outcome or
measurement basis. Save confirmed new facts through the profile helper after review.
While waiting, draft supported portions. Never fill gaps with plausible numbers.

## 3. Select and write the strongest evidence

Choose examples by direct relevance, strength of supporting evidence, ownership and
recency. Prefer distinct examples over several bullets making the same claim. Preserve
chronology within experience; put the strongest relevant bullet first within each role.
An early-career candidate may lead with education or relevant projects; an experienced
candidate may lead with experience. Include leadership/projects only when real and
useful. Do not force empty sections or a summary that merely repeats a skills list.

For each bullet, identify action + object/deliverable + method/context + supported
result. Use only the parts actually known; a precise responsibility or deliverable is
better than an invented achievement. Keep the situation/task implicit when clear.
Distinguish contributed, built, owned and led. Team outcomes must not become sole
individual accomplishments. Do not upgrade exposure to proficiency or a course to
professional experience. Derived figures need candidate-confirmed inputs and meaning;
never borrow a number from an unrelated source just because it exists in the profile.

Synthetic example: source says "Built a SQL report used in the weekly inventory
review." A defensible bullet is "Built a SQL report for weekly inventory reviews."
"Reduced inventory costs by 30%" is unsupported. If impact matters, ask what decision
changed and whether the candidate can substantiate it.

Mirror the posting's terminology only when it accurately describes sourced experience.
Use a common abbreviation and expanded term where helpful and accurate. Never replace
one technology with another, add hidden keywords, stuff repeated phrases, or present
transferable skills as exact experience. JavaScript is not evidence of Java; Power BI
is not evidence of Tableau. Explain transferability in the review instead.

## 4. Review as a reader, then verify the actual file

Use the existing critique fields to assess:

- clarity: can a reader locate the target fit, employers, titles and dates quickly?
- relevance: do the opening section and strongest bullets address the stated priorities?
- evidence: are ownership, scope and outcomes supported, with no inflated language?
- gaps: what essential/preferred criteria remain unmet or unknown, and why?
- improvements: what specific change would improve this version, with cited text?

Record actionable findings with exact resume text, posting quotes or source references.
Unsupported claims, contradictions, unreadable output and broken required content are
blockers. Stylistic alternatives are suggestions, not reasons for endless rewriting.
Make one focused revision for identified issues; repeat only for unresolved blockers
or changed inputs. Do not label a self-review an independent recruiter assessment.

Use clear section labels and readable single-column formatting unless the candidate
needs another format. Keep contact information as text. Avoid decorative icons as the
only labels, skill bars and text boxes that disrupt reading order. Follow the requested
file type or employer requirement. Extract the final file and inspect every rendered
page for clipping, density, split headings, dates and reading order. A Markdown draft
cannot establish rendered pagination or an employer's parsing behavior.

If a page limit needs cuts, show the proposed omissions and their effect on coverage;
keep the complete source profile and follow the candidate's instruction. Do not erase
relevant true evidence silently or squeeze it into illegible type. Run document-add,
document-verify, document-approve and document-attach-check as documented. A rewrite
changes bytes and requires fresh verification/approval; never attach a draft as approved.

## 5. Deliver a compact review, not a marketing score

Provide the final artifact (or clearly labelled draft), the most important changes
and why, and a short requirement/evidence/gap table. Report essential and preferred
coverage separately as counts with the denominator and reasons. These are reviewed
requirement counts, not a hiring probability or ATS pass rate. Do not invent weights
or compare scores using different requirement lists. If comparing an original and a
revision, use the same posting and rubric; show improved evidence placement and wording
without suggesting the candidate acquired new skills. Report missing baseline data.

Optional cover letters and application emails are created when requested or required,
not for every job. Anchor them in one or two relevant sourced examples and verified
company/role context; avoid invented passion, relationships or manipulative claims.
Keep them concise, consistent with the resume and draft-only. Outreach still needs its
own authorization. Interview talking points must use the same verified experiences.

Reuse confirmed facts and approved role-family positioning for efficiency, but reassess
each posting and exact final file. Keep source links and review evidence in the private
workspace via existing helper records, never in distributed skill files. Track actual
tailoring approach and document hashes in attribution. Outcome analysis may suggest an
approved experiment, but small samples do not prove a resume strategy caused interviews.
